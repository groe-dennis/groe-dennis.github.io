## Chapter 2: The Way of the Physical Senses

* In this approach, the basic idea is to anchor yourself in the present moment by focusing on external sight, external sound and physical (i.e., non-emotional) body sensations. 

* Practice for new Monks in Zen Temples

* Related to the pyschological approach "distraction". However, here it is not just a momentary relief but a sustained and systematic apparatus

* Ultimate aim: Experience Oneness with the outside world -> tangible strategy for pulling you out of past, future, and fantasy and into the Power of Now.

* The idea is to use the natural attractive power of external sights to help you stay anchored in the hereand-now. To facilitate that happening, it's okay to intentionally look at pleasant objects or scenes that you enjoy, for example, note See Out while walking in nature.

* If an unpleasant Feel Out sensation (such as pain or fatigue) is present, you may find that your attention is constantly drawn to it. That's okay; simply note it over and over again, pouring openness and acceptance into it each time you note.

## Chapter 3: The Way of Tranquility

* Many, perhaps most, traditional forms of meditation develop concentration power by focusing on simple, soothing experiences. In traditional Buddhism, this is called tranquility (samatha) or absorption (jhana) practice. (In Christianity its called Prayer of Quiet)
-> Creates a positive feedback loop (The more you concentrate on soothing rest, the more soothing it becomes, thereby motivating you to concentrate even more)

* Relative Rest:  approach involves turning towards restful states (through i.e. noting)

* Absolute Rest:  involves dropping the intention to turn anywhere (through Do-Nothing Technique)
